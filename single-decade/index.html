Which Mystery Today?

How I choose the one mystery for my daily single decade of the Rosary — the weekday picks the set, the Church's calendar picks the number, and one small formula does the rest.

One decade, one mystery

Most days I pray a single decade of the Rosary — ten Hail Marys on one mystery — rather than all five decades. A single decade is prayed on one mystery; that is the traditional form of the devotion (splitting all five mysteries across one decade is a modern adaptation). But which mystery? I don't choose by mood, and I don't start over at the first mystery every week. I let the Church's calendar choose, using a simple two-step rule — and because it's a rule, it can be computed for any day of the year.

Step 1 — The weekday picks the set

Which family of mysteries?

The Rosary's four sets of mysteries are assigned to days of the week in Rosarium Virginis Mariae, the apostolic letter of St. John Paul II. That assignment is the first half of the rule:

Day of the weekSet of mysteries
MondayJoyful
TuesdaySorrowful
WednesdayGlorious
ThursdayLuminous
FridaySorrowful
SaturdayJoyful
SundayGlorious — with two seasonal exceptions below

The Sunday exceptions (also from Rosarium Virginis Mariae, which leaves Sundays to the season): on Sundays in the Christmas season, pray the Joyful mysteries; on Sundays in Lent, pray the Sorrowful mysteries. Every other Sunday is Glorious.

Step 2 — The liturgical week picks the number

Which of the five?

Every day of the Church's year belongs to a numbered week — you can see it in the title of the day's Mass readings: "Friday of the Twenty-eighth Week in Ordinary Time," "Second Week of Advent," and so on. That week number is the second input. The Church counts the weeks of each season like this:

SeasonHow the weeks are counted
AdventWeek 1 begins on the First Sunday of Advent (the Sunday nearest November 30).
ChristmasWeek 1 begins on Christmas Day.
LentWeek 1 begins on the First Sunday of Lent (the days from Ash Wednesday belong to week 1).
EasterWeek 1 begins on Easter Sunday; the season runs to Pentecost.
Ordinary TimeCounted from both ends so the year always fits: forward from the Baptism of the Lord (its Sunday opens week 1) until Lent, then backward after Pentecost — the last week is always the 34th, ending the Saturday before Advent begins.

Take that week number — call it W — and run it through one formula:

Mystery number = ((W − 1) mod 5) + 1

"mod 5" means: the remainder after dividing by 5

Why the subtraction and addition? They shift the count so the cycle lines up: subtract 1, take the remainder when divided by 5, then add the 1 back. The effect is a simple rotation that wraps every five weeks — weeks 1–5 give mysteries 1–5, week 6 starts again at 1, week 7 gives 2, and so on, all year long, through every season. No mystery is tied to a particular week; the calendar keeps dealing them out in turn. Here is the whole rotation:

Worked examples

Friday, October 16, 2026 — today
Friday → Sorrowful set · 28th Week in Ordinary Time, W = 28
((28 − 1) mod 5) + 1 = (27 mod 5) + 1 = 2 + 1 = 3

3rd Sorrowful Mystery: The Crowning with Thorns

Thursday, October 15, 2026
Thursday → Luminous set · 28th Week in Ordinary Time, W = 28
((28 − 1) mod 5) + 1 = 3

3rd Luminous Mystery: The Proclamation of the Kingdom

Sunday, December 6, 2026
Sunday in Advent → Glorious set (no exception applies) · 2nd Week of Advent, W = 2
((2 − 1) mod 5) + 1 = 2

2nd Glorious Mystery: The Ascension

Sunday, December 27, 2026 — the Sunday exception
Sunday in the Christmas season → Joyful set (exception) · W = 1
((1 − 1) mod 5) + 1 = 1

1st Joyful Mystery: The Annunciation

Monday, June 15, 2026 — the wrap-around
Monday → Joyful set · 11th Week in Ordinary Time, W = 11
((11 − 1) mod 5) + 1 = (10 mod 5) + 1 = 0 + 1 = 1

1st Joyful Mystery: The Annunciation — week 11 wraps back around to mystery 1

Tuesday, March 23, 2027 — Holy Week
Tuesday → Sorrowful set · 6th Week of Lent, W = 6
((6 − 1) mod 5) + 1 = (5 mod 5) + 1 = 1

1st Sorrowful Mystery: The Agony in the Garden

The four sets

For reference, the five mysteries of each set, in order:

SetThe five mysteries
Joyful1. The Annunciation · 2. The Visitation · 3. The Nativity · 4. The Presentation in the Temple · 5. The Finding of Jesus in the Temple
Sorrowful1. The Agony in the Garden · 2. The Scourging at the Pillar · 3. The Crowning with Thorns · 4. The Carrying of the Cross · 5. The Crucifixion
Glorious1. The Resurrection · 2. The Ascension · 3. The Descent of the Holy Spirit · 4. The Assumption of Mary · 5. The Coronation of Mary
Luminous1. The Baptism in the Jordan · 2. The Wedding at Cana · 3. The Proclamation of the Kingdom · 4. The Transfiguration · 5. The Institution of the Eucharist

That's the whole method: weekday → set, liturgical week → number, ((W − 1) mod 5) + 1. One decade a day, and over the weeks the whole Rosary unfolds in the Church's own order of time.
